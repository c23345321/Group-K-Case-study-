#### HISTORY CHAPTER, to have understanding on the topic.

As mentioned in previous paragraphs, printing was invented as early as the fourth millenium BCE. Since then, printing technology has evolved significantly over the centuries, developing in ways that would have been impossible to imagine in the past.
This development has brougth many benefits to society, especially in terms of education and accessibility of informations. The ability to produce books in increasingly larger quantities has made knowledge and culture more accessible to a wider audience. Historically books were very expensive and the production of a single unit could take up to multiple days, for this reasons a very small percentage of the population was able to write and read.
Before the introduction of printing machines, scribes were responsible for copying books and documents by hand, this process was very inefficient and took a considerable amount of time.
These reasons, combined with the rapid growth of the population, developing new technologies that could improve the speed and quality of printing became a necessity.

#### WEEK 2 work Ethical Reflections.

There are no doubts that the evolution of printing technologies has brought many advantages in terms of productivity and accessibility of information. However, these improvements also raise important questions regarding the relationship between technological improvement and human employment.
The introduction of Laser and 3D printing has changed permanently the skills required in different industries, many tasks became easier and quicker to accomplish, but these technologies also contributed to the gradual automation of tasks previously performed by humans. One of the main ethical concerns associated with technological progress is the replacement of human workers with the newly developed machines. Historically the introduction of printing technologies reduced the need for certain manual tasks, such as copying documents by hand, similarly DOT matrix and laser printers automated the production of documents, allowing many businesses to complete more tasks and with less human intervention. This problem has existed since the industrial revolution, when many workers where quickly replaced with machines that were much more productive than they could ever be.
