##Second viewpoint of printing techonology:analysis
1.
Printing is a way of disseminating knowledge and it's a way of controlling the means of communication too. 
Nowadays words and pictures could be printed and copying did not automatically give everyone the ability to print, 
to buy books or to be able to read books. 
Since the dawn of time, printing has served as a mechanism to defy others and at the same time, government, religion, 
and corporations have used it to disseminate their newsletters and messages.


This strain has been around since early times of the printing press. 
Print in movable type meant letters could be re-used but it still needed presses, materials and people to be invested in. 
Yet Gutenberg's Bible proved a great success in the printing of books in Europe, and opening up books for all did not come with the introduction of the press. 
Print expanded the possibilities for dissemination of knowledge but did not create an equal opportunity to gain knowledge.


The Reformation exemplifies the power of print, on both sides. 
Martin Luther's message was passed on in religious literature and pamphlets,
which weakened the power of the Church over religion. 
The public was also influenced by the printing of Luther's supporters and their critics and also of their political governments. 
The Oxford Research Encyclopedia recounts the use of print as a tool of advocacy for various groups, 
and the attempts of authorities to control or counter what was being disseminated. Judging who had the right side came from no part of the press. 
It was simply a means for rival factions to battle roughly on the same level.


Governments and professional organizations also attempted to regulate the printing and publication of news. 
In the early modern period in England the Stationers' Company were involved in regulating the printing trade. 
In historical research it is important to keep in mind at different times the company's attempts to limit the number of publications 
as well as the company's attempts to limit the power of the gatekeepers of access to the trade, 
and the attempts of the state, which makes it hard to draw up a narrative in which printing as a medium was a factor that diminished the power of gatekeepers. 
There were new possibilities for the dissemination of ideas; rulers and the dominant printers could do the same—and do that themselves.


Printing changed the way information was transmitted in society. 
Easy to make uniform copies of printed books; knowledge could be shared beyond knowledge of those able to read handwritten manuscripts. 
Publishers had made their selection of what to publish and distribute. There would be ancillary text, 
which did reach readers, so that would form individuals' understanding, and other views would not be discussed so much. 
The printing of language and knowledge may also have led to the normalisation of its language and knowledge, 
but the person or the organization who printed a text also decided what would be contained and to whom it would reach.


As Industry had developed in the 1800s, the issue of ownership became clearer. 
The mechanised presses, along with continuous rolls of paper, speeded up the making of paper. 
Tens of thousands of papers per hour were produced by the end of the century by rotary presses that could print, 
cut and fold, a benefit for readers but because of the high cost of the expensive press, paper and distribution network only the more wealthy publishers could afford them. 
It may be impossible for smaller printers to match. The printing work was also influenced by the rapidness of production, some of which was mechanised, but where the emphasis was on the more significant management and use of large machinery. 
The increased mobility of people was beneficial, but also resulted in a more concentrated production and the changing of the roles of workers.


The advantage of the digital / on-demand printing seems to be one of increased control for the individual author. 
Consumers want print books, and the writer can print them; the writer doesn't have to make a big stock, and the risk of ruin and cost are mitigated. 
It's not easy to make a book, though, but it doesn't guarantee readers. 
Digital tools and skills for editing and designing, platforms for promotion and dissemination for authors. 
There are also some barriers to the publishing of which print-on-demand does not completely remove but does ease, such as the fact that books are not distributed evenly or sold.


Also, environmental impacts don't play by the rules when it comes to more: progress is more: faster than he should be able to make and more: cheaper. 
Paper printing consumes resources in various phases of the process from forestry and pulp production to the manufacture, transport and disposal of paper. 
It was determined that the different phases of the paper life cycle air emissions and energy consumption are tied to the environment 
and that if these costs are reduced then either more paper is produced which would result in more paper waste, unless managed properly.


The debate is now taken on a new level with three dimensional printing not just a duplication of information, but the creation of objects. 
It can produce parts from digital designs and can help to minimize material waste in some instances. 
More consistent methods of measuring environmental impacts (EI) are needed, however, as are the people who know how to operate the equipment, a printer, and suitable materials and design files. 
However, publishing the design online does not imply that it is accessible to everyone to be an owner of the object.


This way of thinking reveals that the history of printing has been more than an evolutionary march from the awareness and knowledge of the limited to the empowerment of the many. 
Speakers of each breakthrough impacted on who could make copies or objects, but there were some advantages that included ownership or money, skill, literacy, distribution, and political freedom. 
Spread of ideology and propaganda, censorship, limited resources for certain organisations, environmental costs etc. are some of the drawbacks of the widespread printing of concepts. 
It has opened possibilities for people to create and share what they want to, but without assuring that anyone, anyone benefits, and that anyone pays the costs
